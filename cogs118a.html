COGS 118A // SUPERVISED MACHINE LEARNING ALGORITHMS

Comparing Different Machine Learning Algorithms

Role

Data Analyst, Researcher, & Coder

Research Question

How do supervised machine learning algorithms compare in classification accuracy across varying dataset characteristics and train-test data split ratios?

Timeline

September 2025 - December 2025

01. ALGORITHMS

KNN (K-nearest neighbors)

K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) makes a prediction on an unseen data point by analyzing the proximity and labels of its neighboring instances within the dataset. The applications of KNN are mainly within classification, from recommendation engines to pattern recognition. However, while KNN is easy to implement and adaptable, it doesn’t perform well with high-dimensional data and is prone to over-fitting the dataset.

Decision Tree

Decision trees organize data into a hierarchical framework composed of root, internal, and leaf nodes to evaluate inputs and determine final outcomes. While tree complexity dictates how data points are subsetted, larger datasets face a heightened risk of overfitting compared to smaller ones. Despite this drawback, decision trees remain widely used because they are straightforward to interpret and demand minimal data preparation.


Logisitic Regression

Logistic regression analyzes relationships between independent variables and a categorical outcome, distinguishing itself from linear regression, which predicts continuous values. Depending on the dependent variable, models are categorized as binary for two outcomes, multinomial for three or more unordered outcomes, or ordinal for ordered categories. This framework allows the algorithm to effectively predict the likelihood of specific categorical results occurring.

Random Forest

Random forest relies on bagging—a technique that trains independent models on random, bootstrapped data samples—to build an ensemble of decision trees. By tuning parameters such as node size, total tree count, and sampled features, the algorithm effectively handles both classification and regression tasks. This combined approach reduces prediction variance across the forest to deliver more robust overall outputs.

02. DATASETS

Heart Disease

For our first dataset, we downloaded the heart disease dataset, with variables such as age, sex, and other medical classifications. Through our research, we found that the KNN algorithm works best with the heart disease dataset due to the relationship between the clinical features and the target. The heart disease dataset has many small, mixed clusters of patients, and KNN is highly flexible, meaning it can group these local clusters, capturing changes that most linear models miss.


Iris

From the iris dataset, variables included sepal length and width, as well as petal length and width. Our research seems to find 3 different algorithms that produce the best test accuracy: KNN, decision tree, and logistic regression. We found that the data seems to have nearly a straight boundary, with little room for error or overlap.


Student Performance

Our final dataset was the student performance dataset, with varibles such as age, sex, school, education level, and more. For the student performance dataset, we found that the random forest algorithm works best with the 50/50 partition, while decision trees works better for the 20/80 and 80/20 partitions.


Note: All datasets are part of UCI Machine Learning Repository. Since each dataset has to also be trained with different partitions, we will show the standard 50/50 partition for all of the algorithms, but all of the values will be listed below. (To change the partitions, we change the test size to 0.8 for 20/80 and to 0.2 for 80/20.)

Overall, the machine learning algorithms yielded very high accuracy results from training and testing on 3 different datasets. Looking at only the 50/50 partition, KNN has the best accuracy for the heart disease and iris dataset, while random forests has the best accuracy for the student performance dataset. Looking at the 20/80 partition, KNN has the best accuracy for the heart disease dataset, while decision tree has the best accuracy for the iris and student performance dataset. Finally, looking at the 80/20 partition, KNN has the best accuracy for the heart disease dataset, logistic regression for the iris dataset, and decision tree for the student performance dataset.
