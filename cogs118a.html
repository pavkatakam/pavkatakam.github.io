Timeline

01. ALGORITHMS

KNN (K-nearest neighbors)

K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) makes a prediction on an unseen data point by analyzing the proximity and labels of its neighboring instances within the dataset. The applications of KNN are mainly within classification, from recommendation engines to pattern recognition. However, while KNN is easy to implement and adaptable, it doesn’t perform well with high-dimensional data and is prone to over-fitting the dataset.

Decision Tree

Decision trees organize data into a hierarchical framework composed of root, internal, and leaf nodes to evaluate inputs and determine final outcomes. While tree complexity dictates how data points are subsetted, larger datasets face a heightened risk of overfitting compared to smaller ones. Despite this drawback, decision trees remain widely used because they are straightforward to interpret and demand minimal data preparation.

Logisitic Regression

Logistic regression analyzes relationships between independent variables and a categorical outcome, distinguishing itself from linear regression, which predicts continuous values. Depending on the dependent variable, models are categorized as binary for two outcomes, multinomial for three or more unordered outcomes, or ordinal for ordered categories. This framework allows the algorithm to effectively predict the likelihood of specific categorical results occurring.

Random Forest

Random forest relies on bagging—a technique that trains independent models on random, bootstrapped data samples—to build an ensemble of decision trees. By tuning parameters such as node size, total tree count, and sampled features, the algorithm effectively handles both classification and regression tasks. This combined approach reduces prediction variance across the forest to deliver more robust overall outputs.

03. DISCUSSION

Project Results: Accuracy Comparison

  1. Accuracy (K-means on Full CLIP, D=1024): 68.94%
  2. Accuracy (K-means on PCA Features, D=10): 68.94%
  3. 3. Accuracy (K-means on PCA Features, D=2): 68.94%

All of the accuracies are the same, meaning that a car's left or right direction is prominent to the model. Theoretically, this means that the left/right classes are captured almost entirely by the very first few principal components. However, all 3 of the algorithms having the same accuracy could also be a sign that our model didn’t work as well as we thought.



K-means Clusters on 2D PCA Features

We noticed that the yellow and purple clusters represent the 2 different directions our dataset of car images were facing. Since the clusters are very separate, this shows that the left/right direction is very strong in the CLIP features. The accuracy of 68.94% is very high, but the scatter plot with 2 different purple clusters shows where the 31.06% of errors occurred.
